# Validation Protocol

## Latent Information Dynamics and Endogenous Price Formation in Bitcoin

This document defines the validation, leakage-control, statistical-inference and result-admission protocol for the research programme.

The purpose is to ensure that any reported predictive improvement represents genuinely out-of-sample information rather than:

- look-ahead bias,
- overlapping-label contamination,
- preprocessing leakage,
- repeated specification search,
- hyperparameter overfitting,
- selective reporting,
- unrealistic transaction assumptions.

The central rule is:

> No result is considered evidence unless the information, model and validation process available at prediction time could have existed in real time.

---

# 1. Validation Objective

The primary comparison is

$$
\mathcal{M}_{P}
\quad\text{vs.}\quad
\mathcal{M}_{I}
\quad\text{vs.}\quad
\mathcal{M}_{I+P}.
$$

The primary research claim requires evidence that

$$
\mathcal{L}
\left(
\mathcal{M}_{I+P}
\right)
<
\mathcal{L}
\left(
\mathcal{M}_{P}
\right)
$$

under genuinely out-of-sample evaluation.

The stronger claim requires

$$
\mathcal{L}
\left(
\mathcal{M}_{I}
\right)
<
\mathcal{L}
\left(
\mathcal{M}_{P}
\right).
$$

---

# 2. Validation Hierarchy

The project uses three validation layers.

## Layer 1 — Development Validation

Used for:

- feature research,
- model comparison,
- hyperparameter tuning,
- horizon selection,
- architecture selection.

## Layer 2 — Walk-Forward Validation

Used to measure stability across time.

## Layer 3 — Locked Final Holdout

Used only once the research specification has been frozen.

The locked holdout is not a development dataset.

---

# 3. Chronological Ordering

Let observations be ordered by information time:

$$
t_1<t_2<\cdots<t_N.
$$

Random shuffling is prohibited for primary financial forecasting experiments.

For fold $k$,

$$
\max(\mathcal{T}_k)
<
\min(\mathcal{E}_k),
$$

where $\mathcal{T}_k$ is the training region and $\mathcal{E}_k$ the evaluation region.

---

# 4. Walk-Forward Structure

The preferred development structure is expanding-window evaluation.

For fold $k$,

$$
\mathcal{T}_k
=
[t_0,t_k]
$$

and

$$
\mathcal{E}_k
=
(t_k,t_k+m].
$$

Then

$$
\mathcal{T}_k
\subset
\mathcal{T}_{k+1}.
$$

The forecast sequence is therefore generated by models trained only on earlier observations.

---

# 5. Label Interval

For prediction made at time $t$, define its label interval as

$$
L_t
=
[t,t+h].
$$

Two observations may have overlapping labels whenever

$$
L_t\cap L_s\neq\varnothing.
$$

This occurs whenever the forecast horizon exceeds the sampling interval.

For example, if observations occur every minute and

$$
h=15\text{ minutes},
$$

then adjacent targets share most of their future price path.

This dependence must be respected during both validation and inference.

---

# 6. Purging

Suppose an evaluation observation has label interval

$$
L_e.
$$

Any training observation with label interval

$$
L_t
$$

such that

$$
L_t\cap L_e\neq\varnothing
$$

must be removed where the overlap would allow information from the evaluation period to influence training.

Define the purged training set as

$$
\mathcal{T}_k^{purged}
=
\left\{
t\in\mathcal{T}_k:
L_t\cap L_e=\varnothing
\;\forall e\in\mathcal{E}_k
\right\}.
$$

This is particularly important for:

- overlapping forward returns,
- event labels,
- multi-period targets,
- meta-labels.

---

# 7. Embargo

An embargo may be imposed around fold boundaries when observations immediately adjacent to the evaluation region could remain informationally contaminated.

Let the embargo duration be

$$
\delta_e.
$$

Then observations inside

$$
(t_{end}^{eval},t_{end}^{eval}+\delta_e]
$$

may be excluded from subsequent training where necessary.

The embargo duration must reflect the target and information structure rather than being selected arbitrarily.

---

# 8. Point-in-Time Feature Rule

Every feature must satisfy

$$
X_t
\in
\mathcal{F}_t.
$$

Operationally,

$$
t_X^{available}
\leq
t.
$$

A feature is invalid if any information used to construct it became available after the forecast timestamp.

---

# 9. Timestamp Leakage Audit

Each raw source must distinguish where applicable:

$$
t^{event},
$$

$$
t^{publish},
$$

$$
t^{receive},
$$

and

$$
t^{process}.
$$

The usable timestamp is

$$
t^{available}
=
\max
\left(
t^{publish},
t^{receive},
t^{process}
\right).
$$

The database timestamp alone is not sufficient evidence of availability.

---

# 10. Transformation Leakage

Any learned transformation must be estimated from training data only.

Examples include:

- mean and variance scaling,
- robust scaling,
- PCA,
- autoencoders,
- feature selection,
- text dimensionality reduction,
- imputation rules,
- target encoding.

For transformation $T_k$,

$$
\hat T_k
=
Fit(\mathcal{T}_k).
$$

Evaluation data are transformed as

$$
X_{\mathcal{E}_k}^{*}
=
\hat T_k
\left(
X_{\mathcal{E}_k}
\right).
$$

The transformation cannot be fitted using

$$
\mathcal{T}_k\cup\mathcal{E}_k.
$$

---

# 11. Scaling Example

Suppose

$$
z_t
=
\frac{x_t-\mu}{\sigma}.
$$

For fold $k$,

$$
\hat\mu_k
=
\frac{1}{|\mathcal{T}_k|}
\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}_k}x_t
$$

and

$$
\hat\sigma_k
=
\sqrt{
\frac{1}{|\mathcal{T}_k|-1}
\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}_k}
(x_t-\hat\mu_k)^2
}.
$$

Evaluation observations use

$$
z_t
=
\frac{x_t-\hat\mu_k}{\hat\sigma_k}.
$$

---

# 12. Feature-Selection Leakage

Feature selection is part of the model.

Therefore feature selection must occur inside each training fold.

This includes methods such as:

- correlation screening,
- mutual information,
- Boruta,
- SHAP-based filtering,
- recursive feature elimination,
- regularization-based selection.

The invalid sequence is

$$
\text{Full Dataset}
\rightarrow
\text{Feature Selection}
\rightarrow
\text{Train/Test Split}.
$$

The valid sequence is

$$
\text{Train Split}
\rightarrow
\text{Feature Selection}
\rightarrow
\text{Apply Selected Features to Evaluation}.
$$

---

# 13. Hyperparameter Tuning

Hyperparameter tuning must be nested inside development data.

Let

$$
\lambda
$$

denote the hyperparameter configuration.

Then

$$
\hat\lambda
=
\arg\min_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{CV}(\lambda)
$$

must be computed without reference to the locked holdout.

Hyperparameters include:

- regularization strength,
- tree depth,
- number of trees,
- learning rate,
- neural architecture,
- sequence length,
- ensemble weights,
- event-decay parameters.
